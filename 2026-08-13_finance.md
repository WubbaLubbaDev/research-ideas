## arXiv
### [Large Language Model-Driven Small-Capitalization Trading](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12283v1)
**Authors:** Alireza Kargarzadeh, Nariman Khaledian, Navid Parvini et al. | **Published:** 2026-08-12
**Abstract summary:** LLMs can extract richer signals from financial news than fixed sentiment lexicons. This paper explores feeding such signals into small-cap trading strategies, demonstrating that LLM-derived sentiment improves risk-adjusted returns for less-liquid stocks.
**Idea angle:** An LLM-powered news sentiment tool for small-cap IDX stocks, where analyst coverage is thin and news-driven alpha is more accessible.

### [Calibration Bets on the Past: Post-Training Quantization for Financial Time-Series](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12259v1)
**Authors:** Junyi Ye, Ivy Gateri Wanjiku | **Published:** 2026-08-12
**Abstract summary:** Financial forecasting models developed in full precision often need low-precision inference for production. This paper introduces post-training quantization techniques specifically calibrated for financial time-series data, preserving prediction quality.
**Idea angle:** Quantized financial models enable real-time inference on edge devices or low-cost cloud — useful for building affordable real-time screening tools for retail traders.

### [Regime-Gated Residual Mixture-of-Experts for Cross-Sectional Volatility Forecasting](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12251v1)
**Authors:** Junyi Ye, Gargi Vijay Borde | **Published:** 2026-08-12
**Abstract summary:** Financial volatility is regime-dependent. This paper proposes a mixture-of-experts architecture that gates on market regime information to improve cross-sectional volatility forecasts while maintaining training stability.
**Idea angle:** Regime-aware volatility models could power dynamic position sizing for retail algorithmic traders who can't afford full quant infrastructure.

### [Sectoral inter-dependencies drive the loss of structural balance in signed financial networks](http://arxiv.org/abs/2608.12023v1)
**Authors:** Kartik Dahake, Abhijit Chakraborty | **Published:** 2026-08-12
**Abstract summary:** Signed graphs model cooperation and conflict in financial systems. This paper shows how sectoral inter-dependencies destabilize signed financial networks, with implications for systemic risk monitoring.
**Idea angle:** A sector-level network visualization tool for IDX markets — showing how banking, commodities, and consumer sectors propagate risk to each other.
